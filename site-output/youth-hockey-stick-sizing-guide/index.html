Youth Hockey Stick Sizing Guide

Choose a youth stick that a player can control today instead of buying far too long for growth.

Length and flex work together

A youth stick should reach a comfortable chin-to-nose range in skates and have a flex the player can load without forcing the shot. Excessive cutting makes the shaft stiffer and harder to use.

Parent guide

How to size youth sticks

The biggest mistake parents make is buying a stick that is too long so it will "last." A stick that is too long is hard to handle and can hurt development. Youth players almost always need a softer flex than weight rules suggest.

Three sizing rules for youth players

  • Stick to the chin: with skates on, the top should reach chin to nose height.
  • Lighter flex: youth players need a flex they can load without forcing the shot.
  • Control today: the player should be able to bend and handle the stick comfortably.

When to cut

If the stick is only slightly too long, a small cut is fine. But cutting more than an inch changes the flex noticeably. Let the player test the uncut stick first whenever possible.

Common questions

Youth stick FAQ

How long should a youth stick be?

With skates on, the top should reach between the chin and nose. Forwards tend toward chin height; defensemen may prefer nose height.

What flex should a youth player use?

Youth players should use a flex they can load without forcing the shot. Lighter players often need a flex well below their weight divided by 2.

Should I buy a stick to grow into?

No. A stick that is too long hurts handling, balance and flex. Let the player use a stick they can control today.
