In depth

Measuring in the right stance

Do not measure standing straight. Put on skates, bend your knees slightly, hold the stick in your natural hockey stance, and let the top land where it feels natural. For forwards, the shaft should reach roughly chin height. A player who wants more reach may prefer closer to the nose.

Young players grow too

Youth sticks should not be chosen just to last through a growth spurt. A stick that is too long hurts handling, balance and flex. Let the player use a stick they can control today.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Measuring without skates: the stick will be too long once skates are on.
  • Measuring standing straight: you will end up with a stick that is too long in a skating stance.
  • Ignoring the blade: the blade should sit flat on the ice when you are in your stance.

Common questions

Stick length FAQ

Should my stick reach my chin or nose?

With skates on, the top commonly lands between the chin and nose. Chin height favors handling; closer to the nose provides more reach and leverage.

Do defense players need a longer stick?

Many defense players prefer one or two extra inches for poke checks and reach, but the player must still be able to control the puck comfortably.

How does cutting affect the stick?

Cutting shorter makes the stick stiffer and lighter. Finalize your desired length before committing to a flex.
