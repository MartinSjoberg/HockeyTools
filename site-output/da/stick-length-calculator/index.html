How long should my hockey stick be?

The right stick length helps you skate, handle the puck and shoot with a balanced stance. A common starting point is around the chin with skates on, but height, age, position and personal preference all matter.

Use this calculator before you shop, then confirm the actual stick in your skating stance. A stick that is too long can be hard to control; one that is too short may reduce reach and leverage.

Buying guide

How to choose the correct hockey stick length

Stand in your skates with the blade on the ice and your hands in a natural hockey stance. For many forwards, the shaft reaches around the chin. A player who wants more reach, poke-check range or leverage may prefer closer to the nose.

Hockey stick length buying tips

  • Buy for today’s stance: do not choose an excessively long stick just to grow into it.
  • Remember flex: cutting a stick shorter makes it feel stiffer, which can affect shooting.
  • Check the blade: the blade should sit flat while skating and handling the puck.
  • Position is a preference: defense players often add length, while forwards may favor control.

What to do next

How to interpret the recommendation

Use this measurement to narrow your options, then check the actual stick in your normal skating stance. Body proportions and preference are not captured by height alone.

  • Stand in skates with the blade on a flat surface. Check that the blade sits naturally and that you can handle the puck without changing your posture.
  • Treat the result as a fitting aid, not a required measurement. If you are buying for a child, recheck fit as they grow rather than choosing an overly long stick to grow into.
  • Before cutting, check the finished length and consider the change in flex. Ask a qualified shop to help if you are unsure.

Common questions

Hockey stick length FAQ

How long should my hockey stick be?

For many players, the stick reaches around the chin while wearing skates. Forwards may choose slightly shorter for control, while defense players often prefer extra reach.

Should a hockey stick reach my chin or nose?

With skates on, the top commonly lands between the chin and nose. Chin height favors handling; closer to the nose provides more reach and leverage.

Do defense players need a longer stick?

Many defense players prefer one or two extra inches for poke checks and reach, but the player must still be able to control the puck comfortably.

What size hockey stick does a youth player need?

Use height, age group and actual stance together. Avoid choosing an excessively long stick for growth because it can hurt control and change flex.

Can I cut a hockey stick shorter?

Yes, but cutting makes it feel stiffer. Measure and test the desired length before making a permanent cut.
