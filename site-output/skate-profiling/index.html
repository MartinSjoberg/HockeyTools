Skate Profiling

A skate profile is the blade’s curve from toe to heel. It affects how much blade contacts the ice and how the skate feels underfoot. It is different from the hollow ground across the blade.

04

What matters

Choose the profile that matches your game.

A more rounded profile can feel quicker to turn, while a flatter profile can provide more blade contact and a steadier feel. The result depends on the skater and the blade; profile names are not universal measurements.

Hollow sharpening is a separate choice: it shapes a groove across the blade and mainly changes bite and glide. Use the hollow guide for that adjustment.

ProSharp profile names and types

Official profile names in ProSharp articles

ProSharp’s linked profiling articles from 2015 distinguish profile types from named templates. “Single,” “double,” “triple” and “quad” describe radius combinations; they are not all standalone template names. The articles are historical, so confirm current availability and the exact template with your sharpener.

Four-radius templates

Quad 1 and Quad 2

ProSharp lists Quad 1 as 6-9-12-15 ft and Quad 2 as 7-10-13-16 ft. The article says Quad 2 adds stability and speed.

Three-radius templates

Zuperior S, M, L and V

ProSharp lists four Zuperior variants: S (6-12-26 ft), M (6-13-26 ft), L (6-14-26 ft) and V (6-17-26 ft). The article associates them with different holder sizes and skater groups.

The source describes S for lighter skaters, M for medium-weight skaters, L for heavier skaters and V for goalies or heavier recreational skaters.

Detroit, Toronto, Combo and Win

ProSharp’s double-profile article names Detroit 1 and 2, Toronto 1, 2 and 3, Win 1 and 2, Combo 9-10 ft and Combo 10-13 ft. “Double radius” is the profile category, not one named template.

ProSharp’s single-profile article lists these radii. They describe single-radius geometry, not separate branded model names.

Other established systems

Profiles found outside ProSharp

Manufacturers and pro shops also use their own fit labels and geometry. These options describe broader, documented profile concepts rather than claiming that every shop uses identical measurements.

BAUER system

Custom Adaptive Geometry (CAG)

A BAUER fitting system that adapts blade profile, position and balance to the skater. CAG profile options are supplied as manufacturer-specific codes and may change as new blade models and fitting methods are introduced.

  • Uses skating style and player position as part of the fitting process.
  • Ask for the exact radius or cut chart behind the CAG code.
  • Do not assume a CAG number from another shop is mechanically identical.

Blade geometry

Traditional Radius Profile

A constant-radius curve along the length of the blade. It is one of the clearest baseline profiles and is often described by a radius such as 9’, 10’, 11’ or a larger value.

  • A tighter radius generally creates a more rockered, responsive feel.
  • A larger radius leaves more blade in contact and can feel more stable.
  • Useful for comparing any named profile against a simple curve.

Goalie-specific

Pitch Profile

A goalie-oriented blade setup that changes the pitch or angle of the blade relative to the ice. It can shift weight distribution, edge engagement and ease of post-to-post movement.

  • Used to match stance, butterfly depth and preferred movement.
  • Small pitch changes can alter balance immediately.
  • Requires an experienced goalie fitter or skate technician.

Goalie + specialty

Double-Rocker Profile

A curve with pronounced relief at more than one end of the blade. It is primarily used for specialized movement patterns, especially goalie situations that require short push-offs or frequent lateral movement.

  • Reduces the flat contact area compared with a basic hockey profile.
  • May improve quick pivots but reduce straight-line stability.
  • Not a standard recommendation for every player or position.

What to do next

How to interpret the recommendation

Names such as Quad, Detroit and Superior are commercial labels, not universal measurements. Ask what specific geometry will be applied to your blade; a profile affects the blade’s curve from toe to heel, while the hollow is a separate groove across the blade.

  • Tell the sharpener what you feel on the ice and ask them to check your current profile before recommending a change.
  • Ask what the proposed geometry is expected to change for your balance and skating, rather than choosing by the profile name alone.
  • Keep the hollow the same while testing a profile change. Record your setup and change one variable at a time.

Use the hollow guide to adjust bite or glide separately.

Quad Zero: what the name does—and does not—tell you

Documented: ProSharp’s 2015 article gives Quad 1 as 6′–9′–12′–15′ and Quad 2 as 7′–10′–13′–16′. The 2014 AS 2001 Allpro announcement also names Quad 1. The published Quad article describes Quad 1 as a general performance option and Quad 2 as adding stability and speed; those are the manufacturer’s intended benefits, not controlled comparative test results.

Quad Zero: The cited ProSharp documents do not specify a “Quad Zero,” give it a radius sequence, name its designer, or say when it was created. “Quad Zero” is used inconsistently in retail and sharpening discussion, so do not assume it means Quad 1, Quad 2, or a particular sequence such as 6–9–11–13 ft. Get the exact radii, segment layout, holder-size limits and source template from the shop before comparing it.

Who made it, and why? The official material identifies ProSharp as the publisher and equipment company behind its Quad templates, but does not credit an individual inventor, product developer, patent or interview for Quad Zero. The documented rationale for combination profiles broadly is to combine maneuverability/acceleration associated with a shorter radius and stability/glide associated with a longer one. Applying that rationale to Quad Zero specifically is an inference, not a confirmed origin story.

How four radii work: A radius label is the radius of a circle in the lengthwise side profile, not the hollow across the blade. In a typical sequence, a tighter forward arc can make the front feel more responsive; successive larger arcs can lengthen the effective running surface toward the heel. The exact transition locations and resulting contact depend on template, blade length, holder, setup and how the profile is applied. Four radii do not mean four fixed points are touching the ice.

What the published Quad 1 sequence can tell you (not a Quad Zero specification): ProSharp lists 6′–9′–12′–15′ and its general profiling article describes four functional zones in order from front to rear: acceleration, maneuverability, speed and rear stability. Read cautiously, the four Quad 1 radii correspond to those successive zones. ProSharp does not publish exact zone boundaries or contact lengths, and this sequence must not be relabeled Quad Zero.

Zuperior: the documented triple-radius family

ProSharp’s 25 August 2015 article is the primary source for Zuperior S, M, L and V. It calls the family a three-radius combination, says only the middle radius changes to account for holder size, and targets skilled/offensive players. The article particularly describes S for a power forward, M for medium-weight skaters, L for heavier skaters and V for goalies or heavier recreational players. These are the manufacturer’s 2015 recommendations—not medical, biomechanical or independent test findings.

The mechanics: rocker, contact, pivot and balance

  • Rocker and contact: The rocker is the blade’s lengthwise curve. On a level surface, only a portion of the edge contacts the ice at once; the length and position of that contact patch shift as the skater changes load, ankle angle and blade pitch. A profile name alone cannot specify a fixed contact length.
  • Pivot: The effective pivot is the region about which the blade rolls during a turn or transition. It moves with the skater’s pressure and posture; it is not necessarily the geometric midpoint or a manufacturer-set “balance point.”
  • Balance point and pitch: Profile curvature changes where the blade meets the ice as it rocks, but does not by itself prove a forward or rearward static balance shift. Pitch, holder mounting, boot alignment and blade geometry also matter. Ask the fitter what datum they measure and whether they changed pitch or only rocker.
  • Pressure and force transfer: Body weight and push-off force travel through boot, holder, steel and the two edges. A shorter effective contact patch can concentrate load; the edge’s bite also depends on hollow, sharpening quality, ice and force. A longer patch can distribute load and feel calmer, but does not automatically create more speed.
  • Acceleration and top speed: A more curved front may make it easier to engage the forefoot in short steps and tight turns; a longer running surface may feel steadier in a glide or crossover. Neither creates energy. Stride mechanics, strength, friction, edge quality, hollow and fit can dominate; manufacturer benefits are design intent, not a guaranteed result.
  • Turning, stops and edge work: Turning radius is not the same thing as profile radius. The skater’s lean, edge angle, speed and hollow determine the path. Profile affects how the blade transitions between contact zones and may alter perceived agility or stability. Stops, backward skating and crossovers use changing parts of the blade, so test all of them rather than judging by one straight glide.

Choosing for forwards, defense and goalies

Forwards: A player who values quick first steps, tight turns and rapid edge changes may test a more responsive front section. A power forward may also value stable support in contact along the boards. There is no position-specific universal profile; test starts, crossovers, hard stops and top-speed posture.

Defense: Backward skating, transitions, lateral mobility, pivots and stability while shooting may all matter. A longer rear section can feel supportive to some skaters, but excessive running length can feel less agile. Do not select solely by position or copy another player.

Goalies: Stance, shuffles, T-pushes, recoveries and post integration call for goalie-specific evaluation. Do not apply a player profile recommendation as a default. Profile, pitch and holder/steel geometry are distinct adjustments and should be recorded separately.

Trade-offs and misconceptions: A multi-radius profile is not automatically faster, more stable, or “pro”; four radii do not mean four simultaneous contact points; profile radius is not hollow radius or the skater’s turn radius; a flatter profile does not guarantee top speed; a pro’s setup is not transferable without matching blade, holder, fit and technique; and no cited evidence supports one official NHL profile.

FAQ

Who invented Quad Zero?

The sources cited here do not identify an inventor, product developer, patent or launch date for Quad Zero. ProSharp documents Quad 1 and 2, not a Zero specification. Ask the retailer for the actual template and original manufacturer documentation.

Is Quad Zero the same as 6–9–11–13 ft?

Do not assume so. The cited official ProSharp Quad article lists 6–9–12–15 ft and 7–10–13–16 ft for Quad 1 and 2. It does not define Quad Zero. Confirm each radius and segment layout.

Who developed Zuperior and when?

ProSharp published Zuperior S/M/L/V on 25 August 2015 and describes the template family and intended users. That article does not name an individual inventor or product developer, nor does it state a first conception date.

Does Zuperior make a player faster or accelerate faster?

ProSharp describes intended acceleration, maneuverability, speed, balance and edge control. Those are manufacturer claims, not a guarantee or independent test result. Player technique, fit, blade, sharpening and ice conditions affect outcomes.

Does a longer radius move the balance point forward or back?

Not by itself in a reliably predictable way. Balance depends on what is being measured; rocker, pitch, holder mounting, boot alignment and stance all matter. Ask the technician to state the reference point and measured change.

Is there an official NHL profile?

No league-wide standard is established by the sources cited here. Individual players and teams may use customized setups, but claims about a specific NHL player require a traceable primary source.

Nybörjare, avancerade spelare och elitnivå

Vilka profiler används av avancerade och professionella spelare?

Nybörjarråd: Börja med en standardprofil som ger både balans och en jämn glidyta. Behåll samma skålradie under testet. Om skridskon känns instabil i svängar kan profilen vara för liten eller plötsligt hård; om den känns tung eller svår att vrida kan en annan kurva vara lämplig.

Avancerade råd: Fler-radieprofiler kan utformas för att kombinera olika egenskaper genom bladets längd, men källorna här belägger inte hur många elit- eller NHL-spelare som använder Quad. En Quad-profil är inte automatiskt snabbare eller bättre; kontrollera den exakta geometrin, bladmodellen och skålradien.

Acceleration, toppfart och position

  • Acceleration: En mer rockerad profil kan ge snabbare kantkontakt i start och snäva svängar, men kan minska stabiliteten i rak glid.
  • Toppfart: En längre eller mer plan profil kan ge stabil glid och mer bladkontakt, men kan göra den första puckrutinen tyngre om den är för aggressiv.
  • Forward: Kan prioritera snabb start, snäva svängar och responsiv kantkontroll. En teknisk forward kan vilja ha mer “bite” i ett stängande steg.
  • Center: Många centrar behöver balans i båda riktningar, snabb vändning vid väggspel och stabilitet i stopp. Profil och skålradie testas ofta tillsammans med offensiva och defensiva uppgifter.
  • Back: Kan prioritera rak stabilitet, snabba övergångar i glid och kontroll i varierad fart. En längre profil kan vara användbar vid långa glidturer och öppna ytor.

Elitnivå-rekommendationer och individuell anpassning

På skridskoprofil elitspelare-nivå används profiler som Standard, Quad, Triple eller specialsystem som BAUER CAG. Valet beror på spelarens vikt, fot- och höftposition, bladlängd, istyp, vändteknik och om spelaren behöver mer grepp eller mer glid. Materialaren kan mäta nuvarande kontakt, matchis och bladinställning innan en anpassad profil införs.

Professionella spelare i NHL, SHL och Hockeyallsvenskan resonerar i speluppgifter, inte i marknadsnamn. En SHL-forward som vill ha snabb acceleration kan välja en mer responsiv profil, medan en defensiv back kan prioritera stabil glid. En standardprofil kan fungera bättre för en nybörjare eller motionär som behöver jämn och förutsägbar kontakt.

Vanliga misstag hos elitspelare

  • Att byta både profil och skålradie samtidigt, så att två förändringar blandas ihop.
  • Att kopiera en NHL-profil utan att kontrollera spelstorlek, is, bladserie eller personlig teknik.
  • Att jaga maximal glid utan tillräcklig kantkontroll i stopp eller snäva svängar.
  • Att anta att en äldre eller ny profilnamn automatiskt är identiskt mellan maskiner eller butiker.
  • Att välja en specialprofil för att den ser avancerad ut i stället för att lösa ett konkret problem.

När är en specialanpassad profil motiverad?

En specialanpassad profil kan vara motiverad när en vanlig profil inte löser ett konkret problem: kontroll i svängar, snabba startsprång, obehag i höft eller knä eller en tydlig skillnad mellan vänster och höger fot. Testet ska genomföras med samma skålradie, blad och istyp. Dokumentera förändringen över flera träningar, inte enbart i ett enstaka prov.

Vilken slipning använder SHL-spelare? Det finns ingen officiell SHL-standard. Spelare och materialare varierar skålradie och profil efter is, teknik och position. Begär en uppmätt rekommendation från en kvalificerad skridskoslipare och använd ett jämförbart testintervall.

Nybörjare, avancerad spelare och elitnivå

Nybörjare och motionär: En genomgående, balanserad profil och återhållsam ändring är oftast lättare att lära sig på. Ungdom och junior elit: Profilen granskas efter tillväxt, styrka och spelkontroll. Veteran och senior: Komfort, balans och skydd av kanten kan vara viktigare än snabbhet. Professionell: Anpassningar kan göras i mindre steg och dokumenteras match för match.
